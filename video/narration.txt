When you put money into a shared fund, someone usually holds a key. They can change the rules, pause it, or stop withdrawals. You have to trust them.

This is Plinth, built on NEAR. A shared fund nobody can change.

First, set the rules once. Pick the tokens and the limits. Once it's live, nobody can change them, not even you.

Then, people join by putting tokens in, and get shares for what they add.

A manager, a person or an AI, can only propose trades the rules allow. Anything else is refused.

And anyone can take their share of every token out, at any time.

Behind the scenes, the fund is a small program on NEAR, a public blockchain. It has no key at all, so nobody can change it. Anyone can check that from their browser.

And the best part? We tried to trick it. Two AIs got the same hidden instruction to send the fund's dollars to an attacker. One went along. The other refused, so nothing was sent. A hijacked manager tried too, and was refused on chain.

Plinth. No keys. No hidden switch. Your share, any time.
